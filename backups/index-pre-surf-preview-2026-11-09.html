EXPLORE WAKESCOUT

One topic at a time.

Built for wake surfers

Stop staring at weather apps.

WakeScout turns a pile of forecast data into one useful question: when is the water most likely to be good?

Wind that matters

WakeScout looks beyond a single wind number and compares average wind, maximum wind, and gusts.

Real surf windows

Rain and daylight are part of the equation, because calm water at midnight isn't particularly useful.

Water temperature

Lake-specific seasonal temperature estimates give you a better idea of what you're climbing into.

The philosophy

Useful beats complicated.

What WakeScout does

Uses understandable inputs, compares them consistently, avoids fake precision, and turns the result into something useful on the dock.

What WakeScout doesn't do

Pretend it can overpower mountains, canyon winds, thunderstorms, shoreline geography, boat traffic, or Mother Nature.

Under the hood

How WakeScout thinks

WakeScout doesn't just find the lowest hourly wind. It evaluates every valid 3-hour daylight window, moving forward one hour at a time.

How windows are ranked

Each candidate contains exactly three consecutive hourly readings, at the start, one hour later and two hours later. The full three-hour window must fit between sunrise and sunset. Sunrise rounds up to the next whole hour when needed.

If any valid window has maximum rain probability at or below 30%, windows above 30% cannot win. Eligible windows are then ranked by:

  1. Surf quality
  2. Average sustained wind
  3. Maximum sustained wind
  4. Maximum gust
  5. Maximum precipitation probability
  6. Earlier start

All three limits must be met for each quality label.

Wind and gusts in mph; rain is the highest hourly probability in the window.

The glass problem

“5 mph wind” doesn't tell the whole story.

Average isn't everything

A three-hour window with calm average wind but repeated hard gusts can produce worse water than slightly stronger but steadier wind.

Best available water

WakeScout isn't promising perfect weather. It's trying to identify the best surfable conditions available that day.

Water temperature

Useful estimate. No fake precision.

WakeScout provides estimated water temperatures using lake-specific monthly baselines built from historical observations, seasonal patterns, available lake data, and climatological estimates where direct measurements are limited.

“About what should the water feel like this time of year?”

These are not live buoy readings. Actual water temperature can change with weather, depth, inflow, elevation, turnover, and where you are on the lake.

WakeScout calls them estimates because that is exactly what they are. The web app uses the selected lake’s monthly baseline without interpolation or adjustment for today’s weather. Reports disclose historical, modeled, or proxy provenance. Thirty-six baseline values lack full independent verification and remain explicitly uncertain; they are preserved, not presented as measured observations.

Included companion

Lake Shootout

Sometimes the question isn't when to go. It's which lake deserves the boat today.

Lake Shootout compares multiple Utah lakes using the same WakeScout weather logic and lets the conditions decide where you should surf.

WakeScout checks one lake deeply. Lake Shootout puts several lakes head-to-head. Lake rankings use the same weather criteria. Driving time is only a lake-ranking tie-breaker when available. This web app has no driving-time data, so weather ties use lake name alphabetically; it never invents travel times.

Lake Shootout is included with WakeScout. Lake Shootout installs automatically with WakeScout and should not be downloaded separately. The web app’s multiple-lake forecast requires no installation.

Forecast disclaimer

WakeScout is a planning tool. Forecasts and estimated water temperatures can be wrong, and conditions can change quickly. Always use your own judgment and follow local weather alerts, boating regulations, and safety guidance.

Forecast locations & your privacy

Forecasts use documented park or monitoring points, shown in each report. Conditions vary across large lakes. Weather comes from Open-Meteo; all report times use Utah’s local timezone.

No accounts, trackers, location collection or backend database. Your last lake is saved only in this browser. Weather requests send the selected forecast coordinates to Open-Meteo.

Add the website to your home screen

On iPhone, use Safari’s Share menu, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, use your browser’s Install app or Add to home screen menu. Internet is required for forecasts.

Another way to scout your session

WakeScout, right in Apple Shortcuts.

We’ve also built WakeScout to run in Apple Shortcuts, Apple’s app for running automated tasks on your iPhone. The WakeScout Shortcut guides you through choosing a lake and a day, then puts together a forecast report to help you find your best wakesurf window.

If you already use Shortcuts, it’s a convenient way to plan your next session without opening the website. Lake Shootout is included automatically, so you can also compare lakes without installing a separate companion Shortcut.

What you need

The Shortcut is optional. You can keep using the website in your browser without these Apple requirements. The original Shortcut lists the following requirements:

  • An iPhone running iOS 27 or newer
  • Apple Shortcuts
  • Access to Apple's Cloud Pro model
  • An internet connection for weather, updates, and temperature data
